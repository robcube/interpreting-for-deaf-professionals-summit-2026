    Does it fit?

    “Ready” means parked, checked in and through the 15-minute arrival/preparation block. Assignment times stay fixed. A late arrival does not silently move the client’s meeting.

    What could disrupt the plan?

    • Congestion, a collision or a closure on the selected route. Recheck before each departure.
    • Event traffic around SoDo, a parking search or a building’s security queue.
    • An assignment overrun or a meal that takes longer than expected.

    These are possible issues to check, not reports of actual incidents on September 30.

    What AI can help organize

    A draft schedule, missing buffers, restaurant source links and a fallback plan. A person still verifies the route, confirms dietary needs and coordinates any assignment change.

    This page runs local schedule calculations. It does not call an AI model, retrieve live traffic, make bookings or send messages.

    Travel assumptions and the calculation

    Fictional sites use neighborhood labels only. No client names, exact assignment addresses or appointment details are needed for this public demo. For real routing, use only location information approved for the chosen tool.

    Each driving leg adds a 10-minute contingency. Each assignment adds 15 minutes for parking, check-in and preparation. Ghostfish adds 5 minutes for parking plus the selected ordering/eating time. Packed lunch uses that same selected meal duration at an approved place near Site B before departure.

    If readiness falls after a meeting starts, the schedule is flagged. Assigned end times remain fixed; the page does not claim late arrival is acceptable or predict a rescheduled finish.

    Packed-lunch fallback

    Choose food that meets your own dietary needs and plan suitable storage. Confirm a permitted place to eat near Site B.

    The model removes the restaurant detour but keeps a real meal break. It does not “solve” a late schedule by deleting lunch or arrival preparation.

    Before eating: verify current hours and menu, discuss ingredients and preparation/cross-contact needs with staff, and follow your own dietary requirements. A website’s gluten-free claim is not an individual safety guarantee.

    Presenter walkthrough and source notes
    1. Show the base plan. Ask, “Where might AI help outside the meeting itself?” Pause briefly; no audience device or group work is needed.
    2. Select “Try a 20-minute traffic delay.” Show the missed readiness target at Site B. Ask, “What would you verify or coordinate before accepting this day?”
    3. Switch the lunch plan to packed lunch. Explain that it helps the afternoon but cannot repair a missed morning assignment.
    4. Open the restaurant source and the current traffic source. Distinguish published facts, teaching assumptions and live information that still needs checking.
    5. Open and copy the prompt. A presenter can paste it into any suitable AI tool and compare the sourced result with this saved fallback.

    Muse ≠ Meta AI ≠ Manus

    Muse is Meta’s personal agent. Meta AI is its general assistant. Manus is a separately named agent product.

    An agent may do more than answer. Begin with public or fictional information and a “draft only” task. Inspect permissions in the actual product; a prompt is not a privacy control.

    Choose the tool. Keep the professional judgment.

    • For the day-planning demo: use the same prompt in an approved tool with web research. If browsing is unavailable, require “unverified” labels. Not every tool can generate a webpage or follow the whole prompt.
    • For real assignments: confirm agency/client policy before uploading information or connecting email, calendars or files. A public bio cannot establish a Deaf client’s interpreting preferences or working style; ask them.
    • For travel and food: verify ETAs in a current navigation tool, check official traffic sources and confirm dietary preparation needs with the restaurant. AI search is not live navigation or a food-safety guarantee.
    • For accessibility: look for typed input, readable responses and usable keyboard controls. These tools support preparation; none is being presented here as a replacement for a qualified sign language interpreter.
